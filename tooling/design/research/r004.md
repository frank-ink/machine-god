# Q
What minimal set of semantic palette roles (beyond accent/foreground/background/muted/warning/error) ensures WCAG AA contrast in terminal TUIs across truecolor, ANSI-256, and 16-color fallbacks without per-terminal calibration?

# A
Minimal semantic palette for accessible Ratatui TUIs (2024–2026)

Finding: Six additional semantic roles are a practical minimum beyond accent, foreground, background, muted, warning and error. However, WCAG AA cannot be guaranteed across arbitrary terminal palettes without calibration or control of rendered colors. ANSI colors are user-configurable.

2. WCAG AA requirements

Normal text: ≥4.5:1.

Large text: ≥3:1.

Essential graphical controls and focus indicators: generally ≥3:1 against adjacent colors.

Color cannot be the sole carrier of meaning.

These requirements derive from WCAG 2.2 (W3C, 2023) and its non-web interpretation, WCAG2ICT (2024).

4. Minimal deterministic audit

For each rendering mode:

Resolve every semantic role to its effective color.

Enumerate foreground/background combinations actually used.

Assert WCAG contrast thresholds.

Reject unregistered hardcoded colors.

Snapshot Ratatui TestBackend buffers across themes, sizes and focus states.

Verify that monochrome output preserves selection, focus, warning and error distinctions.

Critical limitation: Without reading or controlling the terminal's effective ANSI palette, contrast calculations validate assumed colors, not necessarily displayed colors.

Recommendation: Implement 12 semantic roles total, audit complete foreground/background pairs, and provide a monochrome accessibility fallback.

Evidence status: Palette abstraction and fallback patterns are implemented in open-source projects; no verified study demonstrates universal WCAG AA compliance across uncalibrated terminals.
